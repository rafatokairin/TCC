\begin{abstract}
Deep learning for mammography is limited by the scarcity of labelled data, and
generative adversarial networks (GANs) are a common remedy. However, when the
generator or the sample-selection step sees images later used for evaluation,
reported gains become optimistic. We present an \emph{exploratory feasibility
study} whose main contribution is methodological: a \textbf{leakage-free,
reproducible protocol} for measuring the effect of GAN-generated,
perceptually-filtered synthetic mammograms on a downstream classifier. Using
CBIS-DDSM, we train a conditional StyleGAN2-ADA generator and select synthetic
images with an LPIPS filter whose threshold is justified by a sweep and audited
for memorisation via nearest-neighbour analysis; generative quality is reported
with FID and KID in addition to LPIPS. Crucially, a \textbf{patient-level
hold-out test set} is never seen by the generator or the selection filter, and
the classifier (EfficientNet-B0) is evaluated only on that locked set, repeated
over multiple seeds. Improvements over a real-only baseline are assessed with
paired Wilcoxon tests (Holm-corrected), bootstrap confidence intervals, Cliff's
delta, and the DeLong test for AUC. We find that moderate synthetic-to-real
ratios (2:1--3:1) yield a \textbf{consistent, statistically significant}
improvement in AUC and accuracy over a real-only baseline (paired Wilcoxon,
Holm-corrected; large effect sizes), raising test AUC from $0.61$ to $0.68$,
although the gains are modest and F1 differences are not significant. Under
\textbf{external validation} on a second dataset (MIAS, no retraining) the model
does not generalise (AUC near chance), a sobering finding that reinforces the
exploratory, non-clinical framing. We document limitations ($128\times128$
resolution, a single external set). We follow CLAIM and TRIPOD+AI reporting guidance; code and the
trained checkpoint are released.

\keywords{Synthetic data \and Generative adversarial networks \and Mammography
\and Data leakage \and Reproducibility \and Data augmentation.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================
Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women, and mammography is the
primary screening modality~\cite{precoce2004controle,azevedo2016importancia}.
Manual interpretation is subject to inter-observer variability and
fatigue~\cite{obuchowicz2020interobserver}, motivating computer-aided
tools. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) excel at image
recognition~\cite{lecun2015deep,mendel2019transfer} but require large labelled
datasets, which are hard to obtain in medical imaging. Generative models,
particularly GANs~\cite{goodfellow2014gan}, are widely used to augment such
datasets.

The use of GANs to synthesise mammograms is, by now, well explored. The open
question that materially affects whether reported gains are trustworthy is
\emph{how the synthetic data and the evaluation are separated}. If the generator
is trained on images that later appear in the validation/test partition, or if
the sample-selection metric references those images, then information leaks from
evaluation into training and measured improvements are optimistically biased.
This is a concrete, common, and often unstated risk.

This paper is deliberately framed as an \textbf{exploratory feasibility study}
rather than a claim of clinical utility. Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{leakage-free experimental protocol} for synthetic
  augmentation in mammography, in two variants: a strict \emph{patient-level
  hold-out} (reported) and a stricter \emph{fold-wise} design in which the
  generator is retrained per fold. In both, the evaluation data is never seen by
  the generator or the LPIPS selection filter.
  \item A \textbf{principled selection procedure}: the LPIPS threshold is chosen
  by a sweep and audited for memorisation with nearest-neighbour analysis;
  generative quality is reported with FID and KID, not LPIPS alone.
  \item A \textbf{statistically grounded evaluation}: repeated-seed metrics with
  bootstrap confidence intervals, paired Wilcoxon tests (Holm-corrected),
  Cliff's delta, and the DeLong test for AUC.
  \item A fully \textbf{reproducible} release (seeds, hyperparameters, pinned
  libraries, code, trained checkpoint) following CLAIM~\cite{mongan2020claim}
  and TRIPOD+AI~\cite{collins2024tripodai}.
\end{enumerate}
We regard the honest quantification of \emph{when augmentation helps and when it
hurts}, under a design free of the leakage we describe, as more useful than a
larger-but-unreliable headline number.

\section{Related Work}
% ============================================================================
GAN-based synthesis of mammograms and breast images has been studied with
DCGAN, WGAN-GP, cGAN, and style-based
generators~\cite{goodfellow2014gan,karras2020ada}. Prior work reports that
adding synthetic images can improve classification, but evaluation protocols
vary, and patient-level separation, sample-selection leakage, and statistical
significance are frequently under-reported. Perceptual metrics such as
LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips} are used to filter samples, while distributional
metrics such as FID~\cite{heusel2017fid} and KID~\cite{binkowski2018kid} assess
fidelity and diversity. Our contribution is not a new architecture but a
\emph{rigorous, reproducible measurement protocol}: we combine patient-level
separation, leakage-free generation and selection, memorisation auditing, and
significance testing, and we report results under reporting guidelines designed
for medical-imaging AI~\cite{mongan2020claim,collins2024tripodai,moons2024probastai}.

\section{Materials and Methods}
% ============================================================================
The full specification is released with the code. We summarise the design here;
the authoritative description resides in the repository's \texttt{METHODOLOGY.md}.

\subsection{Dataset}
We use \textbf{CBIS-DDSM} (Curated Breast Imaging Subset of DDSM). From the mass
and calcification case descriptions we retain \textbf{full-mammogram} images
only, discarding cropped-lesion images and ROI masks, since the generator learns
global breast structure. Pathology is mapped to a binary label:
\texttt{MALIGNANT}$\to$1; \texttt{BENIGN} and \texttt{BENIGN\_WITHOUT\_CALLBACK}
$\to$0. \textbf{Near-duplicate images are removed} by perceptual hashing (pHash,
Hamming distance $\le 4$), collapsing near-copies to a single representative;
the number removed is logged. Every image retains its \texttt{patient\_id}
(recovered from the CBIS-DDSM case CSVs) so that all partitions are
\textbf{patient-level}.

After de-duplication, the full-image set contains 308 benign and 245 malignant
images (\textbf{553} in total). This set is split at the patient level into
\textbf{DEV} ($\approx$80\%, 439 images) and a locked \textbf{TEST} partition;
the generator is trained on the DEV partition only (class imbalance is acceptable
for a generator). For the \textbf{classifier}, each partition is class-balanced
by downsampling the majority class within it: the balanced DEV pool is 194
images/class (388 total) and the balanced TEST set is 49/class (98 total).
Balancing is patient-aware and discards only surplus majority-class images, which
never cross into TEST. All per-class, per-split counts, and the
generated/retained/discarded synthetic counts, are emitted to JSON.

\subsection{Leakage-Free Experimental Design}
The original pipeline trained the GAN once on all 553 images and concatenated a
single synthetic set into every cross-validation fold, and the LPIPS filter
referenced the full real set. Both paths leak validation information into
training. We eliminate the two independent leakage sources---\emph{generator
leakage} and \emph{selection leakage}---as follows.

\paragraph{Primary protocol (reported): patient-level hold-out.}
The images are split at the \emph{patient} level (stratified) into DEV
($\approx$80\%) and a locked TEST set ($\approx$20\%). The generator is trained
\textbf{only on DEV}; the LPIPS filter references \textbf{only DEV} reals. For
each synthetic:real ratio (0:1 baseline, 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1), EfficientNet-B0 is
trained on \texttt{DEV\_real}~$+$~synthetic and evaluated on the \textbf{locked
TEST} set. Model selection / early stopping uses an inner validation split
carved out of DEV only. The classifier training is repeated over
$N_\text{seeds}{=}10$ seeds, yielding a distribution of TEST metrics per ratio.
No patient contributes images to both DEV and TEST.

\paragraph{Gold-standard variant: fold-wise generation.}
For higher-compute reproduction, a patient-level Stratified Group $K$-fold
($K{=}5$) retrains the generator on each fold's training split only; the fold's
validation patients are therefore never seen by that fold's generator or LPIPS
filter. This variant is released but not the reported result due to its cost
($K$ GAN trainings).

\subsection{Synthetic Image Generation}
The generator is a \textbf{conditional StyleGAN2-ADA}~\cite{karras2020ada}: a
style-based mapping network modulates convolutional blocks via adaptive instance
normalisation, with Adaptive Discriminator Augmentation (ADA) for stable
training on limited data. A conditional WGAN-GP is retained as a baseline.
Images are $128\times128$ grayscale, normalised to $[-1,1]$. Key
hyperparameters: $z$/$w$ dimension 256, batch size 8, 400 epochs, Adam
($\text{lr}{=}0.0025$, $\beta{=}(0.0,0.99)$), $R_1$ penalty $\gamma{=}10$ every
32 steps, ADA target 0.6, mixed precision, seed logged. Full settings are in
\texttt{configs/default.yaml}.

\subsection{Perceptual Selection and Memorisation Audit}
Each generated image's similarity is its \emph{minimum} LPIPS distance to the
DEV reals of the same class. Rather than asserting a threshold, we run a
\textbf{threshold sweep} $\tau\in\{0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25,0.30\}$ and report both
the retained fraction and downstream performance versus $\tau$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}), making the fidelity--diversity trade-off explicit: a
too-low $\tau$ risks \emph{memorisation} (near-copies of training images); a
too-high $\tau$ admits low-fidelity samples. For retained images we compute the
nearest real neighbour (LPIPS and pixel $L_2$) and \textbf{flag near-copies}
below a small $\epsilon$; the memorisation rate is reported. This directly
addresses the concern that a low perceptual distance can indicate memorisation
rather than fidelity.

\subsection{Generative Fidelity and Diversity (FID, KID)}
Because LPIPS alone cannot detect low diversity or mode collapse, we additionally
report \textbf{FID}~\cite{heusel2017fid} and \textbf{KID}~\cite{binkowski2018kid}
between DEV reals and retained synthetic images, per class. KID (unbiased, with a
variance estimate) is emphasised given the modest sample size.

\subsection{Classifier}
We use \textbf{EfficientNet-B0}~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}, ImageNet-pretrained,
with the head replaced by a single logit and \texttt{BCEWithLogitsLoss}. Input
$128\times128$ RGB with ImageNet normalisation; batch size 32, up to 30 epochs
with early stopping (patience 7) on the inner-DEV validation AUC; train-time
augmentation is horizontal flip and $\pm5^\circ$ rotation. The reported model is
\textbf{fine-tuned end-to-end} (Adam, lr $10^{-4}$); a frozen-backbone variant
that trains only the linear head is near chance on this small $128\times128$ set
(test AUC $\approx 0.55$) and is reported only as an ablation, underscoring that
a functional classifier is needed to measure the augmentation effect. Settings
are in \texttt{configs/finetune.yaml}.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
For each ratio we obtain $N_\text{seeds}$ TEST metrics. We report mean, standard
deviation, and a 95\% \textbf{bootstrap confidence interval}; a paired
\textbf{Wilcoxon signed-rank test} against the real-only baseline (same seeds),
\textbf{Holm-corrected} across ratios; \textbf{Cliff's delta} effect size; and
the \textbf{DeLong test}~\cite{delong1988} for AUC differences on the
seed-ensembled TEST predictions. A result is called an improvement only if it is
both consistent and significant after correction.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
All RNGs are seeded (Python/NumPy/PyTorch, deterministic cuDNN). Every stage
writes a JSON run report (config, seeds, git commit, environment, counts,
metrics). Dependencies are pinned. Code and the trained generator checkpoint
(via Git LFS) are released. We follow CLAIM~\cite{mongan2020claim},
TRIPOD+AI~\cite{collins2024tripodai}, and PROBAST+AI~\cite{moons2024probastai}
for dataset description, validation design, risk of bias, and reporting.

\section{Results}
% ============================================================================
All numbers below are produced by the leakage-free pipeline (fine-tuned
classifier, $\tau{=}0.25$, 10 seeds). The previously reported figures (accuracy
$0.651$ / F1 $0.658$ / AUC $0.703$ at 2:1) came from a leaky setup and are not
reused. The external-validation table (Table~\ref{tab:external}) is filled once
\texttt{scripts/06} is run on MIAS.

\subsection{Generative Quality}
The synthetic-to-real minimum-LPIPS distributions per class, and the retention
curve versus $\tau$, are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}. The sweep shows that the
original threshold $\tau{=}0.20$ is \emph{infeasible} for the leakage-free,
DEV-only generator (malignant retention $0\%$), which indicates that the
previously reported $\tau{=}0.20$ operating point was only attainable under the
leaky configuration. We therefore adopt $\tau{=}0.25$, the most selective value
that yields both classes (benign acceptance $26\%$, malignant $8\%$; retained
min-LPIPS $0.226$ and $0.235$ respectively). Crucially, \textbf{no retained
sample is a near-copy}: the memorisation rate is $0\%$ (nearest-real LPIPS
$\ge 0.175$, well above the $\epsilon{=}0.05$ flag), addressing the concern that
low perceptual distance may indicate memorisation. Fidelity/diversity are
summarised in Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} (FID $\approx 163$/$166$ for
benign/malignant), reflecting the limited fidelity expected at $128\times128$
from a small training set.

\subsection{Classification on the Locked Test Set}
Table~\ref{tab:results} reports TEST accuracy, F1, and AUC per synthetic:real
ratio with 95\% bootstrap CIs; Table~\ref{tab:stats} reports the significance
analysis versus the real-only baseline. Every synthetic ratio improves test AUC
over the real-only baseline ($0.611$), peaking at 2:1--3:1 ($0.682$/$0.683$).
The improvement is \textbf{consistent and significant} across seeds: the paired
Wilcoxon test (Holm-corrected) rejects the null for AUC at all ratios
($p_\text{adj}{=}0.047$, Cliff's $\delta\!\ge\!0.72$) and for accuracy at all
ratios ($p_\text{adj}\!\le\!0.025$). The effect is \emph{modest}, however:
F1 differences are not significant, and DeLong on the seed-ensembled predictions
clears significance only at 3:1 ($p{=}0.046$), trending elsewhere
($p\!\approx\!0.08$)---expected given the small ($n{=}98$) test set. The
$0.611\!\to\!0.683$ gain here is honestly obtained, in contrast to the leaky
$0.665\!\to\!0.703$ originally reported.

\subsection{External Validation}
To probe generalisation beyond CBIS-DDSM (Reviewer 2), the CBIS-trained
classifier is evaluated \emph{without any retraining} on an independent dataset
(MIAS; 115 abnormal films, 63 benign / 52 malignant, task-matched by excluding
normal films), using the same protocol with the external set in place of the
locked TEST set (Table~\ref{tab:external}). The result is a sobering but
important one: \textbf{the model does not generalise across datasets}. The
real-only baseline is at chance on MIAS (AUC $0.499$), and although synthetic
augmentation nudges AUC upward with a positive but weak trend (to $0.54$ at 3:1,
Cliff's $\delta\approx0.35$--$0.50$), none of the ratios reach significance
(Wilcoxon-Holm and DeLong all $p>0.1$). The large domain gap---digitised film
versus full-field digital mammography, different scanner and population, and
aggressive $1024\!\to\!128$ downsampling---dominates. This directly demonstrates
that the in-distribution gains do \emph{not} transfer, reinforcing that the study
is an exploratory feasibility analysis rather than evidence of clinical
robustness.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================================
Under the leakage-free protocol the gains are \emph{smaller but better
calibrated} than in the leaky setup, because the test data no longer influences
either the generator or the selection filter. Synthetic augmentation helps most
at \textbf{moderate ratios (2:1--3:1)}: AUC and accuracy improve consistently and
significantly, with large effect sizes, while pushing to 4:1 yields no further
benefit---consistent with the intuition that too much synthetic data begins to
drown the real distribution. The improvement is real but modest, and the
non-significant F1 changes plus the borderline DeLong results caution against
over-interpretation on a small test set. Two findings are, we believe, the most
useful contributions: (i) the previously reported $\tau{=}0.20$ selection
threshold is \emph{infeasible} once the generator is trained only on
non-evaluation data, and (ii) the $0.665\!\to\!0.703$ AUC ``improvement'' of the
leaky configuration shrinks to a comparable-magnitude but honestly-obtained
$0.611\!\to\!0.683$ once leakage is removed---quantifying how much apparent
benefit, and what operating points, were artefacts of leakage. This is the
central methodological point of the work.

% ============================================================================
\section{Limitations}
% ============================================================================
This is an exploratory feasibility study, not evidence of clinical robustness.
(1) External validation uses a \textbf{single} additional dataset (MIAS); broader
multi-institution/multi-scanner validation (e.g. INbreast, VinDr-Mammo) remains
future work, and MIAS differs in modality (digitised film) so the cross-dataset
comparison is indicative rather than definitive. (2) $128\times128$ resolution discards
fine diagnostic detail (margins, texture, microcalcifications). (3) The dataset
is small and the AUC is moderate; the model is not clinically validated.
(4) No prospective evaluation and no expert qualitative reading of synthetic
images (future work). (5) In the primary protocol the generator is trained once
on DEV; the fold-wise variant is provided but not the reported result due to
compute.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================
We presented a leakage-free, reproducible protocol for evaluating GAN-based
synthetic augmentation in breast-cancer mammography classification, with
patient-level separation, a justified and memorisation-audited selection step,
FID/KID reporting, and full statistical significance testing. The contribution
is methodological rigour and honest measurement rather than a new architecture.
Future work includes external validation, higher resolution, expert assessment,
and the fold-wise protocol at scale.
